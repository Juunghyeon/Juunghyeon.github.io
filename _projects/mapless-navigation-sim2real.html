01Problem

Same command, different robot, different path

LiDAR-based mapless navigation policies turn range readings and the relative goal directly into velocity commands, and are trained in simulation before they meet the real robot1–5. When wheel–ground friction, suspension response or mass distribution differ between the two, the same command moves them differently6–9, and in closed loop each small deviation changes the next observation and action.

Domain randomization makes a policy robust to such gaps, at the risk of an overly conservative policy10–13. Calibrating the simulator to real data removes them at the source14–16. This work tests calibration on mapless navigation, and judges it not by success alone4 but by how closely the real path follows the simulated one.

02Method

Fit the wheels to the robot, then train

  1. Measure

    Wheel torque and angular velocity, driving straight and turning in place, under six loads.

  2. Tune

    Friction, suspension and other parameters in Isaac Sim, until r ≥ 0.9 and MAE ≤ 0.1.

  3. Train

    The same PPO setup in Isaac Lab, once on the untuned and once on the tuned model.

  4. Compare

    Both policies on the robot, two targets, five runs each, against their simulated paths.

  • output v, ω
  • networks 2 × 256, ELU
  • envs · rollout 256 · 256

Aligned RMSE

RMS distance between points matched by dynamic time warping19,20: where the robot was.

Normalized DTW

Mean distance of the matched points, tolerant of speed differences: how alike the shapes are.

Heading RMSE

RMS heading difference at corresponding points21: which way the robot faced.

04Measurement

Two ways to measure where the robot went

LiDAR scan matching

Each scan is matched to a map of the run's earlier scans (point-to-line ICP). No odometry enters it; the replay above shows these scans.

Wheel odometry

From the wheel encoders of the same runs; it cannot see wheel slip. Over the 20 runs it stayed within 2.78 cm of the LiDAR path on average.

05Limits

Limitations

  • Small sample. Two targets on one indoor floor, five runs per policy and target.
  • No external ground truth. Real paths are LiDAR estimates; a motion-capture check is future work.
  • Not all significant. Target 1's normalized DTW reduction (6.3 %) was not.
  • Estimates differ in size. Odometry shows smaller position gains than LiDAR; heading agrees.
